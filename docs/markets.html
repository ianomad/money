01 / THE TAKEAWAY

The orders are real. The profit on each one is thin.

Worked out on $100,000 bought Dec 31, 2025

  1. 01

    Stocks · fast growers

    Dell turned $100,000 into about $459,930 this year by building the servers AI runs on.

    Most people know Dell for laptops. The money this year came from somewhere else: racks of AI servers, packed with Nvidia chips, that Dell assembles, ships and installs for cloud companies, governments and big businesses. In its quarter ended July 31, 2026, Dell sold $16.4 billion of AI servers, double the $8.2 billion a year earlier. It booked a record $60.9 billion of new AI orders in that one quarter and ended it with $95 billion of orders still waiting to ship. Total sales were $47.0 billion, up 58%, and earnings per share were $6.34, up 273%. Dell then raised its full-year forecast by $25 billion, to $192 billion of sales, including $74 billion of AI servers, about three times last year.

    Dec 31, 2025 close: $125.88 → $100,000 buys about 794 shares
    Low close Jan 20: $111.07 → about $88,235 (it started the year by falling 12%)
    May 29, the day after first-quarter results: +32.8% in one day; the stock doubled in May
    Oct 7, 2026 close: $578.96 → about $459,930 (4.6×, +360%, price only, before dividends)
    Record close Sep 17: $588.40; Oct 7 was 1.6% below it
    A year ago (Oct 7, 2025 close $150.87): $100,000 would be about $383,748

    Why it might not be done: Dell expects $25.50 of adjusted earnings per share this fiscal year, so today’s price is about 23 times that. That is not cheap, but it is far from bubble pricing for earnings growing this fast, and Dell says its pipeline of possible AI deals is still several times its $95 billion backlog.

    The catch: Dell is mostly a builder, not the chip maker. After paying for the parts and making the product, it keeps about 21 cents of each sales dollar (21.1% adjusted gross margin last quarter, 20.9% under standard accounting). Sandisk, this morning’s card, kept about 85 cents. Thin margins leave little cushion if chip or memory costs jump, if big AI customers delay orders, or if rivals cut prices. The stock fell 21% between June 1 and July 29 this year while the business kept growing. The next test is third-quarter results, expected in late November; Dell has not announced the date yet. If you own it, size it so a 20–30% drop doesn’t force you to sell. For more ideas, see Insights; for where to park cash, see Savings.

02 / STOCKS & OPPORTUNITIES

Filing-backed notes.

Plain thesis · risk · scale — not price targets

  1. BRK.B

    BRK.B: $366.3B in cash and T-bills on the June 30, 2026 10-Q.

    Berkshire held $41.4B of cash and restricted cash plus $324.9B of short-term U.S. Treasury bills—about $171 of cash and bills per equivalent Class B share—against $747.9B of Berkshire shareholders' equity (~$348 book per equivalent B share). H1 2026 buybacks were $4.4B. Thesis: a diversified operating conglomerate that is itself parking dry powder in T-bills until it finds a use.

    At scale: Position sizes $50,000 / $250,000 / $1,000,000 buy a slice of that same balance sheet—not a savings APY. Cash-and-bills were 49% of book equity at the report date.

    Risk: Equity and insurance marks move the price. No dividend. Filing figures are as of June 30, 2026; the cash pile and share count change. This is not a yield quote.

    Source: Berkshire Hathaway Form 10-Q for quarter ended June 30, 2026 ↗

  2. SCHD

    SCHD: SEC 30-day yield 3.37% → $33,700 a year on $1,000,000.

    Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index at a 0.06% expense ratio. The published SEC 30-day yield was 3.37% as of Oct 5, 2026 (NAV $32.83 on Oct 6, 2026). Thesis: a low-cost equity sleeve that pays a documented income stream while you keep dry powder in T-bills or insured cash.

    At scale: $50,000 → $1,685/yr · $250,000 → $8,425/yr · $1,000,000 → $33,700/yr
    Same $250,000 in a 0.37% average savings account: $925/yr of interest—and no equity risk

    Risk: Principal can fall; the SEC yield is not a guaranteed distribution. Dividend-focused indexes can lag the broad market for years. Past yield is not a forecast.

    Source: Schwab Asset Management, SCHD fund page ↗

  3. AAPL

    AAPL: $62.1B of buybacks in nine months, plus a $0.27 quarterly dividend.

    Apple's Q3 FY2026 consolidated cash-flow statement (period ended Jun 27, 2026) shows $62.1B of common-stock repurchases and ending cash of $39.5B. The board declared a $0.27 per-share dividend (payable Aug 13, 2026). In the Q2 FY2026 results release, the company authorized an additional program to repurchase up to $100B of common stock. Thesis: large-scale capital return (buybacks + dividend) funded by operating cash.

    At scale: At $50,000 / $250,000 / $1,000,000 position sizes you own a claim on that capital-return machine—not a fixed yield. Dividend income alone at $0.27/share needs a live share price to annualize; use the issuer pages for the current quote.

    Risk: Concentration in one company and product cycle. Buyback pace is discretionary and can slow. Share price can fall even while buybacks run.

    Source: Apple Q3 FY2026 consolidated financial statements and results press release ↗ · Apple Q3 FY2026 results ↗ · Apple Q2 FY2026 results press release ↗
