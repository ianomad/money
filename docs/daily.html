01 / THE TAKEAWAY

The run is backed by sales, and it still nearly halved in five weeks.

Worked out on $100,000 bought Dec 31, 2025

  1. 01

    Stocks · fast growers

    Sandisk turned $100,000 into about $712,958 this year because AI data centers need flash storage.

    AI servers don’t just need chips that think; they need somewhere fast to keep the data. That is NAND flash, the memory inside solid-state drives, and Sandisk makes it. When the AI buildout ran into a shortage of it this year, Sandisk sold more drives at much higher prices. In its quarter ended July 3, 2026, sales hit $8.97 billion, up 372% from $1.90 billion a year earlier. The company said about two-thirds of the jump from the prior quarter came from higher prices and one-third from selling more. Gross margin, the share of each sales dollar left after making the product, went from 26.2% to 84.6%. Sales to data centers were $2.98 billion, about 14 times the $213 million a year before.

    Why it might not be done: for the quarter ending Oct 2, Sandisk guided to $10.3–10.8 billion of sales and $44–46 of adjusted earnings per share. Four quarters at the $45 midpoint would be $180 a share, so today’s price is about 9.4 times that pace. That is cheap for a company growing this fast, and it is cheap for a reason: the market is betting these memory prices are near a peak. The board also added $14 billion to its buyback in August.

    The catch: memory is a boom-and-bust business. In July, memory stocks sold off on fears of new supply: China’s biggest memory maker, CXMT, had a huge stock market debut, and SK Hynix said it would raise 2026 spending on new capacity by 50%. Sandisk fell from $2,335 to $1,015.89 in five weeks even though nothing had gone wrong in its own numbers. If prices for flash stop rising, the profits can shrink as fast as they grew.
