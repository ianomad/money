    Uninvested cash sitting in a Schwab brokerage account earns 0.01%. That is $10 a year on $100,000. 3-month Treasury bills, bought in the same account, pay 4.15% coupon-equivalent — $4,150 a year on the same $100,000. The difference is $4,140 left on the table for doing nothing.

    Schwab idle cash 0.01% → $10/yr on $100,000
    3-month Treasury bills 4.15% → $4,150/yr on $100,000
    Gap → $4,140/yr · Circles on the card are drawn to scale ($4,150 vs $10)

    You do not need a new bank. Schwab already lets you buy Treasury bills in the brokerage. Compare that idle cash rate with the plain savings rates on Savings, and the T-bill tax notes on Insights.

    The catch: A Treasury bill locks cash until maturity (about 13 weeks for the 3-month bill). Selling early gets the market price, which can be less than you paid. Bill yields are coupon-equivalent snapshots, not a bank APY, and they change daily. Federal tax still applies; state tax treatment of Treasury interest is usually better than savings interest, but confirm for your state. Schwab’s cash rate and settlement cash rules can change — check the live cash page before you move money.
