Need $100,000 from your house? Keep the 3% mortgage.

Turning a $300,000 balance at 3% into a $400,000 cash-out refinance at 7.28% costs about $29,120 in interest the first year. Keep the 3% loan and add a $100,000 home equity line of credit (HELOC) at 8%, and it costs $17,000. That is about $12,120 a year you keep.

01 / THE TAKEAWAY

Don’t reprice a cheap mortgage to borrow a little more.

Worked out on $100,000 borrowed

  1. 01

    Home equity

    Got a 3% mortgage and need $100,000? A HELOC on top costs about $12,120 a year less than a cash-out refinance.

    A cash-out refinance pays off your whole mortgage with a bigger new one at today’s rate. So 7.28% doesn’t just land on the $100,000 you take out; it also lands on the $300,000 you already owed at 3%. A home equity line of credit (HELOC) leaves your first mortgage alone and only charges today’s rate on what you borrow. HELOC rates are usually the prime rate plus a margin the lender sets. Prime rose to 7.00% on Sep 17, 2026 after the Fed raised rates, and we used prime plus 1 point, or 8%.

    Cash-out refi: $400,000 × 7.28% = $29,120 of interest in year one
    Keep the 3% loan: $300,000 × 3% = $9,000, plus a $100,000 HELOC × 8% = $8,000 → $17,000
    Difference: $12,120 a year
    Most of it is repricing the old loan: $300,000 × (7.28% − 3%) = $12,840 a year
    Even at 10% (prime up 2 more points), the HELOC route costs $19,000, still $10,120 less
    The HELOC would have to reach about 20.1% before the cash-out refi wins

    Who it is for: anyone who locked a mortgage in 2020 or 2021, when Freddie Mac’s 30-year average sat under 3% for 55 weeks and bottomed at 2.65% in January 2021. The 7.28% is Freddie Mac’s average for home purchase loans from its Oct 1 survey. Cash-out refinances usually cost more than that, since Fannie Mae adds separate pricing charges for cash-out loans, so the real gap is likely bigger. For where to park the cash until you spend it, see Savings; for more ideas, see Insights.

    The catch: a HELOC rate moves with prime, so ask the lender which index it uses, the margin, how often it adjusts and whether there’s a cap. Watch for low intro rates that jump after a few months. Some HELOCs let you lock part of the balance at a fixed rate, usually a higher one. Your house secures it, so if you fall behind you could lose it. On either loan, interest is only tax-deductible if the money goes to buy, build or substantially improve the home. These are first-year interest numbers only; closing costs, fees and principal payments differ, so compare real quotes. Freddie Mac posts a new weekly average today.
