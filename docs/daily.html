01 / THE TAKEAWAY

Debt now costs more than cash earns.

Worked out on $100,000

  1. 01

    Mortgages

    On $100,000, a bigger down payment is a sure 7.28%, about $3,070 a year more than 3-month Treasury bills pay.

    Freddie Mac’s weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed mortgage at 7.28% on Oct 1, 2026, up from 7.03% a week earlier and 5.98% on Feb 26. It last stood that high on Nov 22, 2023 (7.29%). Over the same stretch, 3-month Treasury bills rose only from 3.68% to 4.21%. So every $100,000 you borrow while keeping the same $100,000 parked in Treasury bills costs about $3,070 a year more than it earns. On matching weekly dates, that gap is the widest of 2026; it was 2.30 points in late February.

    30-year fixed mortgage 7.28% → about $7,280 a year of interest per $100,000 borrowed (about $7,250 in the first year as the loan pays down)
    3-month Treasury bills 4.21% → about $4,210 a year on $100,000
    Gap 3.07 points → about $3,070 a year on every $100,000 borrowed instead of put down
    Best Treasury of any maturity today: 20-year at 5.68%, still 1.60 points below the mortgage rate
    Same $100,000 loan at February’s 5.98%: $598 a month; at 7.28%: $684 a month, about $1,030 more a year and $30,900 more interest over 30 years (about $146,300 total)

    Who it is for: anyone buying a home soon with cash on hand, and anyone who took a mortgage at 7% or more in 2023 or 2024, where extra principal payments earn that loan’s rate, guaranteed. For where to keep the cash you do hold, see Savings; for more ideas, see Insights.

    The catch: money in a house is hard to get back without selling or borrowing again, so keep a real cash reserve first. 7.28% is a national average of loan applications; your quote depends on credit, down payment and points. Mortgage interest is deductible only if you itemize, which narrows the gap for some households; Treasury bill interest is taxed federally but not by states. If rates fall later, a smaller loan still refinances fine. Freddie Mac publishes a new weekly number every Thursday at 12 p.m. ET.
