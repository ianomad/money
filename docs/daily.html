01 / THE TAKEAWAY

A low-income year is a tax-free window.

Worked out on $100,000 of gains

  1. 01

    Taxes

    Married with no paycheck in 2026? $100,000 of long-term stock gains owes $0 in federal income tax, instead of at least $15,000.

    The IRS taxes gains on investments held more than one year at 0%, 15% or 20%, depending on taxable income. For 2026, a married couple filing jointly pays 0% on those gains as long as taxable income stays at or under $98,900 (single filers: $49,450). Taxable income is what is left after the standard deduction, which is $32,200 for couples in 2026. So a couple with no wages or other income can take $131,100 of long-term gains and owe the IRS nothing. A couple whose pay already pushes taxable income past $98,900 (about $131,100 of wages on the standard deduction) pays at least 15% on every dollar of gain.

    Married, no other income, $100,000 of long-term gains → $100,000 − $32,200 standard deduction = $67,800 taxable, under the $98,900 limit → $0 federal income tax
    Most a couple can take at $0: $32,200 + $98,900 = $131,100 of gains (single: $16,100 + $49,450 = $65,550)
    Same $100,000 of gains on top of $131,100+ of pay → 15% = $15,000 (up to 18.8%, or $18,800, once income passes $250,000, from the 3.8% net investment income tax)
    Some other income? Each taxable dollar of pension, IRA withdrawal or interest uses up a dollar of room: with $40,000 of IRA withdrawals, $91,100 of gains still owe $0
    Sell and buy right back: shares bought for $50,000 and now worth $150,000 get a new $150,000 cost, so that $100,000 of gain is never taxed later

    Who it is for: early retirees living off a brokerage account, couples on a sabbatical or between jobs, and retirees before Social Security and required IRA withdrawals start. It only matters in a regular (taxable) brokerage account; IRAs and 401(k)s don’t owe capital gains tax on trades. Buying the same shares back right away is fine here, because the wash-sale rule only blocks losses, not gains. For where to keep cash from a sale, see Savings; for more ideas, see Insights.

    The catch: shares held one year or less, including recently reinvested dividends, are short-term and taxed like wages, so choose which shares you sell (brokers call them lots). The $98,900 limit counts all taxable income, so overshoot by $10,000 and that $10,000 is taxed at 15%. States can still tax the gain: California has no lower rate for capital gains and taxes them as ordinary income. More income can also shrink ACA marketplace health-insurance tax credits, make more of your Social Security taxable, and raise Medicare premiums two years later, so run the numbers or ask a tax pro first. Trades must happen by Dec 31, 2026. These are 2026 amounts; the IRS has not yet published 2027’s.
