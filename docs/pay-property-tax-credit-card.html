PROPERTY TAX / CHECKED OCT 26, 2026

Paying property tax by credit card: when the fee is worth it.

California counties charge 2.22%–2.3% to pay property tax by card; eCheck is free. A plain 2% card loses money on that trade: on a $10,000 installment in Alameda County, the fee is $230 and you get $200 back (-$30). It only pays when the card earns more than the fee, when the bill unlocks a sign-up bonus, or when you are in a tight spot and need a few weeks.

Installments are due Dec 10 and Apr 10. Debit cards get charged the same percentage in most of these counties, so paying by debit card is the worst option: you pay the fee and earn almost nothing.

CASE 1 · THE CARD EARNS MORE THAN THE FEE

A 3% card clears every county fee; a 2% card clears none.

Robinhood Gold Card pays 3% on everything, but it needs a Robinhood Gold membership ($60/yr in our data). On a $10,000 installment that is $300 back against a $222–$230 fee. Only count it as a win if you already pay for Gold; buying the membership just for this eats most of the gain on a single installment.

CASE 2 · THE BILL UNLOCKS A SIGN-UP BONUS

This is where card payment really pays: a few dollars of fee can unlock a few hundred dollars of bonus.

If a new card wants a minimum spend you would not hit with normal spending, putting just that much of the tax on it costs the fee on the minimum only. CASE 3 · YOUR CASH EARNS INTEREST A FEW WEEKS LONGER

The float is real but small: $30–$60 on $10,000, enough to turn a 2% card from a small loss into about break-even.

Paying by card on the due date means the cash leaves your account at the statement due date instead, roughly 25–50 days later depending on when your statement closes. At the best plain savings rate we track (4.4%, Pibank), that is $30–$60 of extra interest. Added to a flat 2% card in Alameda (-$30), you land around +$0 to +$30: basically a wash for the hassle. If you would carry the balance even one month, card interest wipes out every case on this page.

HOW TO DECIDE

Default to eCheck. Use a card only for a bonus minimum or a card that earns over the fee.

  • Opening a new card soon: put exactly the bonus minimum on it, eCheck the rest.
  • Already pay for Robinhood Gold: 3% beats every county fee here.
  • Flat 2% card, no bonus in play: skip it; you pay to use the card.
  • Short on cash before the deadline: a card avoids the late penalty, but only if you can pay the statement in full.
