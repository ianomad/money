Negative = the local currency falls. The 1% FX cost is our assumption.

Check the numbers: amount above $0, rate 0–100%, term 1–60 months, move above −100%.

At 44.5% a year for 1 month, the deposit earns 3.7% in TRY over the whole term (the annual rate is not what you get unless the term is 12 months).

Break-even: you lose dollars if the TRY falls more than 1.6% over the term, and you do worse than US savings if it falls more than 1.3%.

Assumptions. Simple interest paid at the end of the term (no compounding inside the deposit); the rate is annual, so a 3-month deposit earns a quarter of it. Money converted to local currency at the start and back to dollars at the end. US comparison: Pibank at 4.4%, held for the same term. Before tax; US taxpayers owe US tax on foreign interest and may need to file FBAR/FATCA reports.

How it works

This covers 10 tracked countries, not the whole world. The highest headline rate here is Turkey at 44.5% (32 days).

Why Kyrgyzstan gets extra detail: we track 8 of its banks, more than any other country here. It is not the highest rate on this list. The KGS moved +1.7% against the dollar from Oct 12, 2023 (NBKR official USD/KGS) to Oct 30, 2026; that says nothing about what it will do next.

Scenario bars: the headline rate for 12 months as simple interest, converted to local money and back with a 1% cost each way, under the currency move shown. “Past pace” uses the average yearly move since each country’s start date. These are what-ifs, not forecasts.

LOOKING BACK · KYRGYZSTAN (HINDSIGHT, NOT A PREDICTION)

$500,000 in a som deposit, October 2023 → today

Converted at the NBKR rate of 88.92 KGS/USD on 2023-10-12, compounded yearly for 3 years, converted back at today’s 87.45. The som moved -1.7% against the dollar, so the currency barely hurt. We don’t have a verified 2023 rate sheet yet, so these are scenarios, not what a bank actually paid then. Kyrgyz rates on the table today top out at 14.5% (Capital Bank, 24–36 mo). The US baseline is 4.27% for 3 years = $566,824.
