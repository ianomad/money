T-BILLS VS SAVINGS / CALIFORNIA / OCT 26, 2026

T-bills vs high-yield savings in California: the state tax decides it.

Treasury bill interest is exempt from California income tax; savings interest is not. In the 9.3% bracket, the best plain savings rate we track (4.40%, Pibank) keeps 3.99% after state tax, while a 26 week T-bill at 4.36% keeps all of it. That is $37 a year more per $10,000, and a savings account would need about 4.81% to match.

THE CATCHES

Bills win on rate, savings wins on access. Split by when you need the money.

  • Access: savings is same-day. A bill is locked until it matures unless you sell it at a brokerage (at market price); at TreasuryDirect you wait.
  • Rate moves: a bill locks today’s yield for its term; a savings rate can change any day, in either direction.
  • Itemizers: state tax you pay on savings interest can be deducted federally if you are under the SALT cap, which shaves a bit off the bill’s edge.
  • Teaser savings rates: a temporary promo rate can still beat a bill while it lasts. See best no-strings savings for the plain rates and the catches.

Keep your emergency fund in savings; put cash you will not touch for 1–6 months into bills.
